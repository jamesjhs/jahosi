Tasker

Tasker is a simple timer and record-keeper for your working day. Each time you start a piece of work, you tap a button — Tasker starts the clock. When you finish, you tap stop and choose a couple of options to describe what the task was. Over time you build up a picture of where your time goes.

Everything is kept anonymous. The system never asks for your name, and no patient information is ever stored. It is designed to be quick and easy to use.

Problem and goal

Administrative workload can be hard to discuss because teams often rely on memory, anecdote or a few unusually busy days. Tasker was designed to make that work measurable without turning staff into subjects of surveillance.

Solution and progress

The solution is deliberately low-friction: start a timer, stop it, categorise the work, and move on. The screenshots below show the development of the capture and reporting flow, with the product goal of producing useful workload evidence while keeping individual users anonymous.

Implementation

The implementation focuses on fast data entry, anonymous records, task categorisation and reporting views. The important design choice is restraint: collect enough structure to understand workload patterns, but avoid names, patient details or anything that would make the tool feel punitive.
